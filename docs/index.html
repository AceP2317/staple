Where repeat visits come from

On today's trucks, a missing part causes 67% of repeat visits?. Washers are worst: 29 of every 100 washer jobs need a second visit because a part was not on board.

Which parts to carry

The predictor ranks parts by how often they fixed the same model family and problem, and leans? on the wider appliance group when a family's history is thin. Three parts on the carry list? cover 71.6% of held-back jobs; the national top three cover 40.3%. Shuffling the history drops the predictor to 38.5%, so the gain comes from what the history links, not from the method. The list tops the truck up for the day; it does not replace the truck.

What to load tonight

Each night the balanced truck ranks every part by its worth per unit of space: the booked calls' odds of needing it times a repeat visit, plus a share for calls that arrive unplanned?, minus what holding it costs?. It covers 90.3% of jobs for $109 a truck-day?, against 78.6% and $180 for today's fixed stock, and it holds less: $3,068 on board against $3,162.

Who takes the call

Every held-back day is replayed with the same made-up sick calls, breakdowns and priority changes. Staple sends each call to the technician with the lowest expected cost: drive and repair minutes, plus a second visit times the chance that technician's skill or truck fails it. Within each priority, the call with most to lose if its best technician fills up goes first?. Shuffling the technicians' skills drops it to 83.3%, below the parts-only rule's 84.1%, so the skill gain comes from knowing who is good at what. A rolled call? counts as not fixed.

Reading the customer's words

Claude Haiku 5.5 (claude-haiku-5-5) turns the customer's own words into a problem code with a confidence. It agrees with the booked code? 92.6% of the time, against 48.2% for keyword rules. When it is unsure it asks the customer one short question, which lifts the 3-part list's coverage from 68.2% to 69.9%; random replies to the same question drop it to 66.2%. Its answers are cached in the repo, so these pages rebuild without a key.

Back-orders to chase first

For each back-ordered part?, the list forecasts the calls that will need it before it arrives, from training history only, and how many of those have no truck nearby holding one. Over the held-back months it expected 84 calls and 14 stuck; the days then brought 90 and 20.

What this shows, and what it does not

It shows the method can be checked: every result here reproduces from one seed, and each gain has a shuffled control that falls back when the history's links are broken. It does not show the gains, because the history is invented. On real history I would run the same back-test first, then a one-region pilot against the current way of loading trucks. Three corners are cut on purpose: counting instead of a trained model, zone-to-zone drive times instead of road routing, and a call-by-call night plan instead of a solver.
